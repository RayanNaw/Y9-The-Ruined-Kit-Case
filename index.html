Key words
Enzyme
A protein that speeds up a reaction and is not used up (biological catalyst).
Substrate
The molecule the enzyme works on.
Products
The smaller molecules made when the substrate is broken down.
Active site
The part of the enzyme the substrate fits into.
Specific
Each enzyme works on one type of substrate.
Complementary
Shapes that fit together exactly, like a key and its lock.
Optimum
The temperature where the enzyme works fastest.
Denatured
Heat changes the active site’s shape for good, so the substrate can’t fit.

Discover · alone, then with a partner · 10 min

What is an enzyme?

An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a chemical reaction without being used up. Enzymes are biological catalysts.

Made by living thingsYour body makes them. So do the bacteria used to make bio powder.
Break big into smallThey break large molecules into small ones, just like in digestion.
Not used upThe same enzyme works again and again.

Picture it: an enzyme is like a pair of scissors. It cuts a big molecule into small pieces, and afterwards the scissors are still there, ready to cut the next one.

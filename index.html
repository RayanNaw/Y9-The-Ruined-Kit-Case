Discover · alone · 4 min

What is an enzyme?

An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a chemical reaction without being used up. Enzymes are biological catalysts.

Made by living thingsYour body makes them. So do the bacteria used to make bio powder.
Break big into smallThey break large molecules into small ones, just like in digestion.
Not used upThe same enzyme works again and again.

Picture it: an enzyme is like a tool that cuts a big molecule into small pieces. After cutting, the tool is still there, ready to cut the next one. Your stomach uses the enzyme protease to break down the protein in egg. Bio powder uses the same enzyme on egg stains.

Model answer (open when your teacher says)

Your kit stayed stained because the enzymes stopped working. Biological powder contains protease, which breaks down protein in the egg and blood stains. At 90 °C, the enzyme is denatured: its active site changes shape, so the protein no longer fits. I recommend washing the kit at 40 °C because test 1 and the graph show protease works fastest at 40 °C (100%) and has 0% activity at 90 °C.
